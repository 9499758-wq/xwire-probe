> GLM-5.4 is a good case study in how Looped Transformers can work at 750B scale.
> 
> Here's the recipe:
> 
> 1. First, @Zai_org took the pretrained GLM-5 base and turned it into a looped model through continued training.
> 
> 2. Then they looped the middle of the stack.
> Layers 1–26 run once, 27–54 run twice with shared weights, and 55–78 run once. That's 106 layer passes per token from 78 layers of weights.
> 
> 3. The team had to make 3 engineering changes:
> 
> - They added per-token "damp gates" to prevent activation blowup: Pass 1 writes smaller updates (more for harder tokens) to build context, while Pass 2 opens wider to refine it.
> 
> - To keep memory under control, Pass 2 reuses Pass 1's sparse KV for global context and keeps its own KV only for a 128-token sliding window. So at 1M context, the extra pass costs one small window instead of a second full cache.
> 
> -  Pass 2 also reuses the sparse-attention indices from Pass 1's indexer, that's why indexing adds no extra cost and accuracy stays almost the same.
> 
> So, does this looping pay off? Z. ai tested 30B variant of GLM-5. more training compute:
> - With ~10% math, science, and long-context scores improved.
> - and output length on GPQA-Diamond and MMLU-Pro dropped 10–14%.
>
